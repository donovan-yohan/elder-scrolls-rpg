CONDITION SYNTAX: explaining how conditions, condition levels, and stacking works.

*   
  * What are conditions?

  Conditions are labels that can be applied to characters, items, or the environment. Conditions represent changing circumstances that can help or hinder characters as they go about the world. Conditions can be applied by a wide range of mundane or supernatural means, and it’s normal to have several conditions applied to any given character at once. It is advised to keep a list of a conditions on hand, with check boxes to track condition levels.


  * What are condition levels?

  Many conditions in *unnamed elder scrolls game* can be experienced at varying levels of intensity. The descriptions for each condition will clarify how increasing the level of a condition effects the affected party. 

  Sometimes, it is possible to apply a condition multiple times, and “stack” them to create a higher condition level. If a conditions description says levels DO stack, then almost any source of that condition will stack with any other source. If a condition description says levels MAY stack, it is usually up to GM discretion whether one source of a condition stacks with another. 

  Any source of a condition will include a number next to the condition applied, to signify how many levels of the condition are applied at once by that source. for example a spell that inflicts “burning 2” gives the affected party two levels of the burning condition. if the spell has a condition level maximum of four, casting this spell twice on a creature would give them 4 levels of burning.


  * What are condition level maximums?  
* Sources of any condition which can be stacked will have a “condition level maximum“ next to the condition itself, shown as a number in square brackets. This number represents the highest level that this source of the condition can apply via stacking. For example, a spell that applies burning 1 \[MAX 1\] could be used to inflict 1 level of burning, but would not increase the affected character to burning level 2 because it’s condition level maximum is level 1\. Conversely, a spell that deals burning 1 \[MAX 3\] could be cast multiple times on a character that is already burning to increase the condition level as high as 3\.  
*   
  * Ending conditions   
* Generally, conditions end when whatever source that applied it runs its course. If a spell applies burning 1 for two rounds, the condition ends after two rounds. Some conditions will have specific situations that cancel them out. For example, the chilled condition is canceled early by the burning condition, and the burning condition is canceled early by the wet condition.

CONDITIONS 

*   
* BURNING (1-4) your body is lit on fire. Burning is a condition with multiple (1-4) levels of severity. multiple sources of the burning condition can stack with each other, but they will always designate the maximum level of severity they can inflict. characters inflicted with the burning condition receive fire damage at the beginning of their turn, and whenever they are inflicted with fire damage from another source. The amount of damage inflicted by the burning condition depends on its severity. The burning condition ends if the flames are smothered, or when the subject receives the wet, chilled, or frozen condition. On their turn, a character may drop prone and spend half of their total AP to reduce their burning level by 1\.  
* Level 1: 1 fire damage   
* Level 2: 3 fire damage   
* Level 3: 6 fire damage   
* Level 4:10 fire damage.

*  BALANCE NOTE: if the bonus fire damage is too high, or is too easy to stack, players are just going to spam fire attacks. The most common sources of fire damage should have a maximum stackable level of one, (torches, novice fire spells, common fire enchantments) max stackable Level two Is for intermediate gear, some spells, and environmental hazards like fire pots. Level three is for very rare spells/equipment. Level four only shows up in special circumstances.

* CHILLED Your body is cooled and slows. Characters inflicted with the chilled condition receive Slowed 1 \[max 2\], And may receive the frozen condition if they are wet (see the Wet condition). The chilled condition ends immediately if the subject receives the burning condition.

* SLOWED Your movement is hampered and your body slows down. Slowed is a condition with multiple levels of severity (1–4). Multiple sources of the slowed condition may stack with each other, but they will also designate the maximum level of the condition they can inflict. Characters inflicted with Slowed 1-3 have their AP costs for movement doubled, and maximum AP reduced by a certain amount.   
*   
* Level 1: your AP is reduced by ¼, and you AP cost for movement are doubled.   
* Level 2: your AP is reduced by one half, and you AP cost for movement are doubled.  
* Level 3: your AP is reduced by ¾, and you AP cost for movement are doubled.  
* Level 4: your AP is zero and you cannot move your own power.   
*   
* Design note. Slowed 4 is basically a stand for paralysis. Earlier games in ES allowed you to stack damage speed effects to basically freeze someone in time. The goal is to have a single condition that can be referenced for cold effects, speed damage, paralysis, time Fuckery, etc. (edited)

ALACRITY your body and senses both become faster. Alacrity is a condition with multiple levels of power (1–4). Multiple sources of alacrity may stack with each other, but they will also designate the maximum level of the condition they can inflict. 

Characters under the effect of alacrity gain additional AP to spend on actions. And are able to spend less AP on movement during their turn. When you calculate the AP costs for movement on your turn, reduce the total sum of your AP costs by a Percentage amount determined by your level of alacrity. 

Characters with 1 \- 3 levels of alacrity may treat   movement from immediate to short range as if it were one distance increment, costing only one AP.

Lvl 1 Your AP is increased by 25%. The total sum of your AP costs For movement are reduced by ¼. 

Lvl 2 your AP is increased by 50%. The total sum of your AP cost for movement are reduced by ½.

Lvl 3 your AP is doubled. The total sum of your AP costs for movement are reduced by ¾.

Lvl 4 if time is a river, it freezes for you. Creatures that have achieved four levels of alacrity May move about freely while time appears to stand still.   
For the duration of this effect, All other beings on your plate of mundus or oblivion are treated as suffering from Slowed lvl 4, and you may treat all of the characters as if they have zero initiative. Physical objects or phenomena also freeze in space unless acted upon by you. You cannot be perceived or detected by any mundane or magical means, Nor is it possible to take reactions to your actions.   
Characters may ignore the effects of another character achieving Alacrity lvl 4 if they themselves have achieved four levels of the same condition. Creatures that have achieved alacrity lvl 3 may move and perceive their surroundings, but act as if they instead had three levels of slowed. 

**Design note**. This is a stand In for fortify speed that also covers time stop and elemental fury. Basically an inversion of slowed. acceleration 4 is time stop.

FROZEN Characters, afflicted with the frozen condition become covered in unwieldy chunk of ice. They may fall prone, or even begin rolling downhill, if standing on an uneaven surface. Frozen characters receive Slowed 4, and all sources of bludgeoning damage are tripled. If a character dies while frozen, their body breaks apart into icy chunks, and cannot be used for the creation of undead. GM’s are encouraged to inflict bludgeoning damage on frozen targets who roll down steep slopes.

WET characters receive the wet condition when submerged in liquid or subject to heavy rain. While wet, characters suffer double cold or electrical damage, but receive half fire damage. 

If a character receives the chilled condition while wet, They must succeed on a DC10 athletics or acrobatics check or gain the frozen condition. If the source of the chilled condition has a spellcasting DC, roll against that instead.

PRONE characters who have dropped or fallen prone spend triple their AP costs to take movement actions. Melee attacked made against prone characters receive \+2 advantage, while ranged attacks are made at \+2 difficulty. Prone characters on their turn may spend one AP to stand up and end the condition. 

At the GM’s discretion characters going prone may become lightly obstructed (see concealment), and cone or line  shaped attacks may be made at \+1 difficulty. 

FALLING Characters who go into a freefall and crash down a solid surface take falling damage dependent on the number of range increments traveled. When calculating falling damage, consider how many range increments were traveled, and Add together damage from each one.   
Immediate \-\> close — 0 damage   
Short — \+2 damage   
Medium — \+2 damage   
Long — \+6 damage   
Extra long — \+8 damage 

PROPOSED FALL SPEED RULES: when a character goes into freefall, the distance they travel is represented by range increments, which add up during the progression of the characters turns.

When a character goes into freefall and it is not their turn, they immediately fall one range increment (immediate \-\> close range).

When a character goes into freefall during their turn, they immediately fall one range increment (immediate \-\> close range), then at the conclusion of their turn they fall in additional increment (immediate \-\> short range). 

When a character starts their turn in a freefall, They immediately fall one range increment,  then fall a second range increment when they’ve expanded half of their AP for the turn, and a third increment at the conclusion of their turn.  

EXAMPLE: a bandit is shoved off a wall where the drop would be a “medium distance” in terms of range increments. It isn’t their turn, so they immediately fall one range increment (immediate \-\> close) accruing no fall damage but picking up some speed. At the beginning of their turn they fall another increment, accruing \+2 damage potential fall damage. The bandit uses half of their AP trying to grab the wall to arrest their fall, but fails. With have their AP expended, the bandit falls another range increment ( now immediate \-\> medium) and hits the ground, taking the combined 4 points of damage. If they are alive, they may use the other half of their AP to complete their turn.

FLYING Flying may either be a condition applied to a creature, or an inherent part of its physiology. Characters with flight as part of their physiology will have the \[inherent\] tag next to their flight level. characters with the flying \[inherent\] tag cannot have their flight ended or modified as it were a condition. They are also immune to the flight condition if its level is lower than their inherent flight level.   
Flying is a condition with multiple levels of power (1–5). Multiple sources of the flying condition may stack with each other, but they will also designate the maximum level of the condition they can apply. Characters affected by the flight condition may lift off the ground And move in three dimensions whenever taking move actions. The level of the characters flying condition determines the cost of AP for movement while flying through the air.   
Level 1 AP cost for movement is four times larger   
Level 2 AP cost for movement is two times larger   
Level 3 AP cost for flight is the same as movement   
Level 4 AP costs for movement are halved while flying   
Level 5 AP costs for movement are quartered while flying.   
Generally, characters under the flight condition can take these move Actions in any environment (such as underwater). creatures with the flying \[inherent\] tag may have this option limited by their physiology.

Design/GM note Obviously, level four flight shouldn’t really be available until at least late second act. Level five flight is pretty much only for dragons and God shenanigans. Level one is strictly for utility and apprentices, level two is for utility and a bit of combat maneuvering. Level three is the best that most wizards can manage, level four is master wizard shit.

CONCEALMENT**:** concealment is a condition which may be applied to a location, an individual for the purpose of hiding, or an individual where conditions would affect their sight. Concealment is a condition with multiple (1-4) levels of severity, which can increase or decrease under specific circumstances as described below. 

Regardless of other circumstances, all locations have a concealment level reflecting the local lighting conditions.

**Level 1** sunny day, clear visibility.

**Level 2** dusk. Diminished visibility. Stealth skill checks by concealed individuals are made with \+1 advantage. Search and notice skill checks are made at \+1 difficulty. Targeted spell and ranged weapon attacks are made at \+1 difficulty against targets at long or longer range.

**Level 3** nighttime, illuminated by the stars or bioluminescent fungi. Poor visibility. Stealth checks by concealed individuals are made at \+2 advantage. Visual Search and notice skill checks are made at \+2 difficulty. Targeted spell and ranged weapon attacks are made at \+2 difficulty against targets at short or longer range.

**Level 4** Pitch darkness, complete blindness. Typically only found deep underground. Stealth checks by concealed individuals are made at \+4 advantage. Visual Search and notice skill checks are made at \+4 difficulty. Targeted spell and weapon attacks are made at \+3 difficulty. AP costs for movement are doubled, and acrobatics checks are made at \+3 difficulty while moving in level 4 darkness.

Various sources can add or subtract levels from a locations base concealment level, either for an individual character or an area of space. 

**Candles, torches, lanterns, mage lights** creates a circle of light that grants \-2 concealment within short range of the light source, and \-1 concealment within medium range.

**Heavy rain fog or snowfall** \+2 concealment level within affected area 

**Light obstructions (bushes, tall grass, crowds of people, noonday shadows)** \+1 concealment level for affected individuals

**Heavy obstruction (pools of water, hidden trenches, deep shadows**) \+2 concealment while observing out of your hiding place. you can receive \+3 concealment from heavy obstruction if you fully hide your body without trying to observe your surroundings. You Suffer level three blindness while hiding in this way.

**Constructed concealment** characters may use the hide skill, or (situationally) the naturelore skill to conceal themselves or a small location. The character spends 10 minutes constructing their hideaway, then attempts to pass a DC 15 check. Success grants \+1 concealment. Rolling a 20 on this check grant \+2 concealment, and rolls of 25+ Grant \+3 concealment. 

**Magical concealment** supernatural effects, such as the chameleon spell, may artificially add concealment levels. The amount is determined by the power and source of your spell. 

BLINDNESS blindness is a condition with multiple levels of severity (1-3).  Blindness does not automatically stack with itself, but at the GM’s discretion multiple sources of blindness may have a cumulative effect. Some possible sources of blindness may be pocket sand, wearing a bucket on your head, a called shot with a weapon at your eyes, a witch’s curse and more.

Level 1 you perceive your surroundings at concealment level 2  
Level 2 you perceive your surroundings at concealment level 3  
Level 3 you perceive your surroundings at concealment level 4

SLOWFALL is a special condition that can be received by creatures or objects, which modifiers the degree to which they are threatened by gravity. In some rare locations (a niche plane of oblivion for example) it may occur naturally as a result of weaker gravity. 

Characters with slowfall as part of their physiology will have the \[inherent\] tag next to their slowfall level. characters with the slowfall \[inherent\] tag cannot have their slowfall ended or modified as it were a condition. 

Slowfall is a condition with multiple levels of severity (1-3). Multiple sources of slowfall can be stacked to produce a higher tier of slowfalling. 

Lvl 1 Gravity affects you slightly less than other beings. You fall at the same speed as other creatures, But do not fall prone or take damage on impact.  

Lvl 2 Gravity effects you only half as much as other beings. While in free fall, you travel half as far as you normally would, and take no falling damage on impact. If you are able to push off a solid surface before or during your fall, you may move a distance horizontally equal to your distance fallen vertically.   
Characters with the Flying condition or the Slowfall 2 \[inherent\] tag may move themselves horizontally in freefall without pushing off of objects. 

If you are thrown horizontally in a given direction (Willingly or by a hostile effect such as a dragon shout) and cannot brace yourself against the environment, you will fall the same distance horizontally in that direction at the beginning of your turn until you stop yourself or the effect ends.

Lvl 3 gravity is suspended for you. You do not fall    in open air, and can move through the environment by pushing off objects or larger creatures. 

If you are thrown in a given direction (Willingly or by a hostile effect such as a dragon shout) and cannot brace yourself against the environment, you will continue to fly in that direction at the beginning of your turn until you stop yourself or the effect ends. 

ENCUMBRANCE   
encumbrance is a special condition that can be acquired through mundane or magical means, representing a change in the characters overall weight.   
encumbered is a condition with multiple levels of severity (1-4), multiple sources of encumbrance can be stacked to create a greater tier of encumbrance.

Mundane applications of the encumbered condition typically come from characters choosing to carry heavy weights, such as a body or a chest full of gold. When this happens the gm may apply a number of encumbrance levels that they think reflects how much the object will  slow you down. it is also possible a mundane poison could sap a character strength and effectively give them encumbrance levels.

Magical sources of encumbrance directly make the target heavier, or increase the degree to which they are affected by gravity. This is commonly caused by spiteful alteration mages, or traps deep in dungeons. 

At the gm’s discretion, levels of magical or mundane encumbrance may be temporarily reduced by shedding weight. For example, a warrior afflicted with burden 2 might go down to burden 1 by abandoning their armour.  

Lvl 1 AP costs for walking are doubled. You suffer one level of disadvantage on skill checks to traverse weight sensitive terrain, such as a frozen lake, rotten floorboards, or pressure plates. You receive \-1 levels of flight.

Lvl 2 AP costs for walking are tripled. You suffer two levels of disadvantage on skill checks to traverse weight sensitive terrain, such as a frozen lake, rotten floorboards, or pressure plates. You receive \-1 levels of slow fall, and \-2 levels of flight.

Lvl 3 you cannot walk with three levels of encumbrance. You receive \-2 levels of slowfall, and \-3 levels of flying. You suffer 4 levels of disadvantage on skill checks to traverse weight sensitive terrain, such as a frozen lake, rotten floorboards, or pressure plates.

Lvl 4 subject becomes 10 times heavier and immediately rolls a dc20 athletics check against falling prone. This roll is repeated at the beginning of the targets turn and any time they attempt to stand up while the effect persists.   
If you are on weight sensitive terrain, you breakthrough immediately. Slowfall / flying effects are both suspended for the condition’s duration (including those with the \[physiological\] tag), And falling damage is multiplied by 4\. Walking is not possible with 4 levels of encumbrance. 

SICKENED is a blanket condition that is attached to a variety of biological ailments, such as being poisoned by food, bitten by a venomous spider, or even nausea from vertigo. Most sources of the second condition also deal damage, but poison damage is dealt with separately. Typically creatures immune to poison are also immune to the sickened conditions accompanying the poison. 

Sickened is not a condition with multiple levels, but more potent sources of illness may come with multiple effects.

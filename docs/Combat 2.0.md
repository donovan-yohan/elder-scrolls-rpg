\-Each character in the party rolls initiative contribution.

Adding \+1 Advantage for each of the following skills that they possess as a major skill

**Notice**

## Your turn

### Attacking

Spend ap to attack. Very simple. You roll your skill, if it beats the opponent’s ac, it hits and deals damage. Your opponent may attempt a reaction to defend themselves. After the reaction is over, your turn may resume if you still have ap to spend.

Yes, your weapons deal full damage, you can kill people quickly still. But what about mister dagger???? Daggers are special, they deal the difference in their attack Vs AC as extra damage, anytime the attacker has at least one advantage on the attack. So try to get that advantage\!\!\!

#### Getting advantage

1. If you are the first to act in combat (and it’s the first turn) you get 1 advantage for all attacks on that turn  
2. If you are under the effect of potions or spells that give you such, drink up and throw a coin to your mage\!  
3. You spend a point of your team's initiative score, you gain advantage on your attacks that turn\!  
4. The opposing team has lost initiative, if you act on that surprise turn, it acts as the first round of combat again, giving advantage as in example 1\.  
5. You have made your foes disoriented, or they are unaware of your presence for any reason.

#### Focused attack

Sometime their amour is just too  much to do much to your opponent, so you need throw your all into the next attack to cut them down. Spend 1 AP to roll your weapons check with a DC equal to 10 \+ your weapons focus cost. For If you beat that DC, your next attack, should it hit, deals double base damage. 

Should you miss a focused attack, you become disoriented. You cannot make a focused attack while disoriented, and must spend 2AP to Resteady. This takes no roll.  
If you critically succeed, all consecutive attacks this round with the same weapon, deal double damage. Each of these attack rolls have advantage.

If you critically fail, you become disoriented, and the opponent team gain one initiative in addition to your party’s normal losing of one.

#### Hand-to Hand Combat

A character may engage in hand-to-hand combat assuming they have at least 1 hand free. You roll to attack with your Hand-to-Hand skill against the opposed AC as in normal melee combat. However, your fists as a weapon have these special rules below. 

1 AP may be spent to do an equal amount of Damage, assuming your hit. If you are unarmoured, you always count as doing blunted damage, which is nonlethal (unless you are a khajjit) and your quality is always considered lesser than an opponent's armour. If you are wearing armour however, you deal bonus damage per attack, and the base AP cost to attack increases. See the equipment charts for details. The quality of your attack is now the quality of your armour against the opponents. The maximum bonus damage you may do with hand-to-hand attack is equal to your Hand-to hand skills bonus.  

#### Attack Tiers

This one is simple. If your weapon is a higher tier than your opponents, you deal your damage normally. If it is the same or lower, your damage is halved. 

### Hefting

Shields are heavy, and to get ready to effectively block might take work. You cannot use a shield to block as a reaction if you have not hefted it. A light shield requires 0 AP to Heft, medium 1, and heavy, 3 ap. You only need to heft a shield once per turn. 

### Limbering

You can no longer ready a dodge on your turn, what you can do is convert 3 AP, to 1 initiative point, for any party member to use. (assuming they can see or hear you) Roll an Initiative skill.  DC:8+parties current score.

## Movement

Distance is calculated loosely in the game with the distance ranges within being merely examples for most cases. Starting at closest, progressing to farther away.  
**Immediate- Being within the same space as a target** (Useless for most melee aside from daggers,used for hand to hand and some specific spells)

**Adjacent- Being within 1-5 feet of the target** (Necessary for most Melee combat and all unarmed combat)

**Close- Being within 6-10 feet of the target** (Just outside adjacent for most close range spells, and pole arms)

**Short- Being within 11-25 feet of the target** ( Good for slings, firearms, and almost all combat spells)

**Medium- Being within 26-40 feet of the target** (Only for Bows, slings,  crossbows, and specific spells)

**Long- Being within 41-85 feet of the target** (only for bows, Crossbows and very specific spells)

**Extreme- Being within 85-200 feet of the target** (sniping range only at feasible distance, this range is mostly the edge of combat distance as any farther away, you are out of combat in most scenarios) 

**Movement action-** You may attempt to change your distance to a given target.   
1 AP to move from Immediate to Close or Close to Immediate or anywhere in between.   
1 AP to move from Close to Short or short to Close.  
2 AP to move from Short to Medium or Medium to short.   
2 AP to move form Medium to long or long to Medium.  
3 AP to move from Extreme to Long or Long to Extreme

| Distance | Effects |
| :---- | :---- |
| **Immediate** | Melee attacks other than Dagger or Hand-to-Hand Take Disadvantage, ranged attacks are not possible. Only specified spells can be cast, and even then, the caster takes disadvantage. |
| **Adjacent** | Melee and Hand-to-hand attacks made normally, Ranged attacks and spell attacks are made With disadvantage. |
| **Close** | Melee and Hand-to-hand attacks are impossible, save for Weapons with the Reach or Large Description. Large weapons attack with disadvantage at this range but can attack.  |
| **Short** | Only Ranged weapons can attack from this distance, as well as most combat spells. |
| **Medium** | Only Ranged weapons may attack from this distance, as well as most combat spells. Attacks made from this distance, take \-5.  (1) |

## Swap weapons/Items

Assuming you have a scabbard, Bandolier, or other specialized gear to keep an item or weapon at ready. You can swap all accessible items in both hands with items in accessible storage for 1 AP. 

If the item you are storing does not have a place to stow quickly, it can either be dropped, for no additional AP, or stored safely (assuming you have a place to store at all) for an additional 1 AP. The same goes if the item you are storing has quick storage on your person, but if the item you’re retrieving is not easily accessible, this also costs 1 additional AP. 

Picking up an item can be part of a move action, provided your hands are already free, and the item is within the space you have moved or along the path to it. Giving an item to a character (who has hands free and willingly accepts it) can also be done for no additional cost as part of the movement action to reach them.

## Death and Dying

If you are reduced to zero or Fewer HP, first check if you have any remaining spirit points. If so, You MUST spend it, and return to full AP, MP and HP and take one complication. 

To take a complication, roll 1d10. Than reduce that stat’s current Maximum by one. On a 1-3 reduce HP, a 4-6 reduce AP, a 7-9 reduce MP, and a 10 is a piece of readied/equipped, equipment or weapon. This equipment becomes damaged until fixed.

Damaged weapons have permanent 3 disadvantage when used, and if enchanted, becomes discharged.  Damaged Armour when worn, gives half it’s AC bonus, and half it;s dodge bonus. You take 2 did against doing any movent skill while wearing damaged armor. If it was enchanted armor of equipment, it becomes discharged.

 To be stabilized, another character must make three successful Nursing checks. The DC 10 \+5 per number of unresolved complications.  This check, if done in combat, takes 4AP to perform. A character must be treated shortly after being downed, and will be considered killed if not nursed back to health within 5 rounds. This can be extended by any character giving them a health potion, or successfully using a Restoration spell on them) This extends their time to be treated by another 5 rounds. 

Nursing must be done with a Healers kit, that takes use of both hands. A healer kit is expended after 5 uses, success or failure, and must be restocked in town before it can be used again.   
If you do not have a stocked healers kit, or one at all, you may still attempt stabilize check, but they are made at \+ 10 difficulty. A failed check does nothing, more than waste a use of a kit and the healers AP.  The Character attempting to nurse the other back to health, gives advantage to all opposing charter attacking them if they are not otherwise engaged.

Upon reawakening, you must take a complication. The player may describe what that complication is (a stutter, deafness, losing a leg) that the DM agrees is appropriate for the level of complication.

A complication is a semi-permanent reduction to one of your character's core stats.  A d10 is rolled, 1-3 Being HP, 4-6 being AP, 7-9 being MP, and a 10 is a piece of readied/equipped, equipment or weapon.  These reductions can only be removed, once the appropriate narrative action has been taken to remedy them. (A potion brewed to stop constant ringing in the ears, a magical prosthetic built to give movement back, a sanctified soul gem to replace a lost eye) Once the actions have been taken, the maximum stat reduction is removed, but the Complication, and their resolution stay on the sheet for later.  
You may take more than one unresolved complication before your character is considered killed entirely (though even then, there are some ways past death if you can find them) For your first complication, you take on one point of reduction. The second complication, while the first is unresolved, you take two points. Then three, and so on. When you can no longer take every point of the complication, your charter is considered dead.

## Reactions

\-When someone is attacked, they may reduce their party's initiative counter by 1 to take a following action.

\-Blocking the attack  
\-Dodging the attack  
\-Cast a reaction spell

You may not take consecutive reactions. You take one, then the opponent responds if they still can, then you may respond if you still wish to.

### Blocking

Is now only done as a reaction.  
You roll your skill to block the attack as before using the combat relevant skill.

Roll and check. If  you match or exceed the attack, you only take 1 damage. If you exceed the attack by ten or more, your party regains 1 initiative counter.

Using any other skill than blocking, gives disadvantage. (spells do not apply here) You must be appropriately equipped to block.

Brawling is acceptable, but you must be armoured  
One-handed is acceptable, but you must be wielding a one-handed weapon.  
Blocking the skill can be used even if untrained.

Equipped with a one handed shield- give your block advantage, a medium shield 3 advantage, and a heavy shield 5 advantage.

So, if you are untrained in blocking, but have a heavy shield, it still might be better to block, than to try and block with a sword.  
If you succeed (with an equal or stronger material) you take no damage.  
If you succeed with inferior equipment, you take 1 damage.  
If you fail by less than 10, you take half damage.  
If you fail by more than 10, you take full damage.

If you crit, you take no damage, and decrease opposition parties inactive by 1  
If you crit fail, you take full damage and decrease your parties initiative by 1

Most spells cannot be blocked this way (i.e a fireball, or acid spray, though a summoned sword can be blocked) If your shield or sword is enchanted with some form of spell resistance or absorbtion, then you may attempt the block, though DM’s discretion prevails. Blocking a lighting bolt with a spell absorption dagger is very difficult.

To block spells normally, Use wards as reactions. Wards act as if they are shields summoned in just a moment to block only spells (not weapons) Wards save up in useful ness (active like tried shields)

### Dodging

Now cost no ap\!\!\! So cool, but only a reaction now :((((

If you are being attacked, and you want to gtfo, you may attempt to do so.

Acrobatics is the perfect skill to use for a Dodge roll.  Athletics may also be used, but rolled with 1 disadvantage. 

Add your armour bonus or penalty to the roll.

If your roll matched or exceeded the attack, you take no damage and move up to 10 feet away.  
If you fail by less than 10, you may choose to take half damage OR become disoriented.  
If you fail by more than 10, you take full damage.  
If you critically fail, you take full damage and become disoriented on your next turn.

## Spellcraft

### Basic casting

When you cast a spell, you pay the AP cost. Than you roll the spells skill and if you match or beat the spell casting Difficulty, you spend the MP cost and the spell is cast. If the spell is a targeted attack spell (or simply a targeted spell that a creature does not want to be hit by) this spell roll, must also beat the creatures AC. (Touch ac, should I bring that in? ### Concentration Spells

For most spells, once you pay the MP and AP cost, you must then roll The correct spell caster check to see if you can cast successfully. If you spend the cost again, you must, again, roll. Concentration spells, though often weaker, can be cast as many times as your stats allows, with only one caster check needed, so long as the casts are consecutive. If you spend any AP on another action for any mean, you must re-roll your caster check to resume the spell.

### Spell Focusing

As in regular combat, 1 AP can be spent to focus to empower your next spell.   
You may increase the Damage/Healing, Duration or range in this way. 

To do so, Roll your spells' appropriate skill check, With a DC equal to the spell’s current power \+ an amount equal to effect desired(see table below). If this number is more than five times it’s base MP, increase the base DC as if the spell’s power was increased by one. 

For example; Flames is a novice spell (base DC 10\) with an MP cost of 1, and base damage of 1\. If you focus intending to add 4 extra damage, the DC is increased to 14\. However if you wanted to increase it by 5 (five times the MP cost), then the base dc of ten would increase to 15 and the \+5 would be added atop for a total of DC20.)

| DC Increase |  |  | DC Increase |  |
| :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- |
| Linear to damage desired | **Damage increase** \+1 \*Up to your Level |  | \+2 | **Duration increase** \+1 \*up to Half your level |
| \+1 | **Range Increase** Immediate to Close |  | \+5 | **Summon Stat Empowerment** \+2 to one base Stat (AP/MP/HP)   |
| \+1 | **Range Increase** Close to Short |  |  |  |
| \+2 | **Range Increase** Short to Medium |  |  |  |
| \+3 | **Range Increase** Medium to Long |  |  |  |
| \+5 | **Range Increase** Long to Extreme |  |  |  |

If you succeed, you now gain advantage casting the spell you focused on fo the rest of your turn, and deal the additional damage/effect. However, the spell costs MP to cast based on it’s base power. (Novice \+1MP focused, Apprentice \+2MP, Adept \+3, Expert \+5MP, and Master \+10MP)

If you fail a spell focus check, you become unfocused. If you are unfocused by this, or other means, you take disadvantage of on all spellcasting checks and must spend 1 AP and 1 MP to refocus to clear this effect. You must refocus before you can attempt a focus check again. This cost does not require a roll.

**Magic Burn \-** There are several ways to accrue the plague that is magic burn, but if you attempt to cast a spell, (and you have at least 1MP) but are still short, you may choose to attempt a cast still. If you succeed in the cast, you manage to draw from your own vital essence to fuel the spell, taking the remaining MP as irreducible Magika damage. It can only be removed via a Cure Magicka Burn Potion or other Spell OR through one point being reduced per night well rested(with food and shelter etc)

**Magicka Regen \-** Spells cost MP, but do not refresh their MP bar at the start of every turn like AP does. MP recovered naturally overtime for most at a rate of half your level rounded down (min 1\)

**Spell Resistance \-** Some races state play with spell resistance of varying kinds, or may gain it temporarily through magic items or potions. Spell resistance is a flat number that is added to your AC against a spell that would trigger the correct resistance. Spell resistance may be added to dodge rolls against Spell attacks.

**Spell absorption \-** However you may gain this effect, spell absorption is a far more powerful version of spell resistance. Spell absorption is given in percentages, and simply, when target by a cast spell, or enchanted item, you take the percent your spell absorption says, less of the effects, and absorb that percentage of the Magika cast, rounded down.

**Wild Spell \-** As long as you have at least 1 AP remaining, but you do not have enough AP to use your spell normally. You may make a wild spell in its place. For every AP missing from the total amount you’d usually need, you gain one level of disadvantage.
